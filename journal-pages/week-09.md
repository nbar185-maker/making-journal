**Drafting with NOtebookLM**

*I have designed a physical landscape model that visualises the environmental impact of our everyday design practices. By transforming discarded Polylactic Acid (PLA) 3D printing filament into sculptural, natural forms such as trees and plants, I have created a tangible representation of a future where "waste" is no longer hidden but serves as a visible building block for our surroundings. The data driving this work is sourced directly from the PLA recycling bins in our university design lab, where I collected and weighed filament scraps over several weeks to understand the scale of material accumulation in our own environment. This project envisions a future university design space where a low-power AI system monitors material waste, categorising scraps as reusable and suggesting new methods for their use. In this scenario, materials are stored and made visible, allowing students to reflect on their material impact and strengthening personal responsibility. I engage critically with data representation through "data physicalisation," where the size and volume of each structure in the landscape directly correspond to the weight of the collected waste. By acknowledging that a significant amount of material is often "lost" to general waste bins rather than being recycled, the work also highlights gaps in our data and the unintentional actions that distance us from sustainable practices. Ultimately, I want to encourage you to rethink the materials you treat as waste. By making this data physical and relatable, I aim to foster a sense of awareness regarding the consequences of material consumption. This work challenges the audience to see discarded scraps not as rubbish destined for a landfill, but as valuable resources that hold the potential to be repurposed into something meaningful*

**Note on underdeveloped elements:** 

*Note on underdeveloped elements: The specific technical integration of the "low-power AI system" into the physical model remains a conceptual part of the future scenario rather than a functional component of the current prototype. Additionally, the practical methods for breaking down and reshaping the collected PLA scraps into the final sculptural forms are still being refined*

**Note**

*Visualising an AI-powered system was originally proposed when my project focused on multiple types of material waste. As the project has become more focused on PLA waste, this idea has also changed. I later proposed creating a physical model made from recycled scraps that includes a sorting system where people can contribute their own waste. This represents a significant shift from the original AI-based concept, which was intended as an imagined future system rather than a realistic outcome. Implementing such a system would require large-scale industrial capabilities that are beyond the scope of this project. Instead, I am focusing on creating an artefact that engages design students directly and encourages them to think differently about PLA waste, making the project more relevant and effective within its intended context.*

**Evaluation**


**What is working well?**

*Focusing on PLA waste from the design lab has given my project a clear direction. My weekly data collection process is working well, and using natural forms like trees and plants makes the environmental impact easier to understand.*

**What is missing or underdeveloped?**

*The biggest area that still needs development is the process of turning the PLA waste into the final sculptural forms. I need to continue testing different methods to see what works best. The final scale of the model also needs further consideration so that it accurately represents the collected data. While the project originally included more interactive elements, it has become more focused on the physical sculpture, so I need to think more about how audiences will engage with the work.*

**What feels overly generalised or AI-like?**

*The visualisation of the data is still somewhat generalised. While I know I want to represent PLA waste physically, I have not fully determined the specific methods I will use to translate the data into form. For example, I am still exploring how weight, volume, scale, and shape will relate to the collected data and how these visual elements will communicate the story effectively. Developing a clearer system for representing the data will strengthen the overall project.*

**What do you need to research further?**

*I need to research how to process and reshape PLA waste, how to better represent my data, and the properties of PLA to make sure it can be used safely. I will mainly do this with help from the design lab technicians through testing and discussion.*

**Write one sentence that commits to the direction of your project.**

*I will create a physical landscape model that transforms PLA waste data into sculptural forms to make material consumption visible and encourage more sustainable design practices.*
